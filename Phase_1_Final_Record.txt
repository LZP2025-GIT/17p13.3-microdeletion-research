                                              Phase 1

            Locus Characterization and Structural Definition of the 17p13.3 Microdeletion

1. Research Objective

Phase 1 establishes the structural genomic foundation for the larger investigation of an

intersti-

       tial 17p13.3 microdeletion and its possible downstream molecular, biochemical,

neurological, and visual-system effects. The index deletion was reported as:

GRCh37/hg19 chr17:1,411,408–1,841,103

       The objectives of this phase were to determine:

1. the exact genomic interval being studied;

2. all identifiable genes and other annotated loci intersected by that interval;

3. which protein-coding genes are fully versus partially deleted;

4. whether the two breakpoint-intersected genes, INPP5K and RTN4RL1, lose actual

protein-

       coding sequence;

5. the relationship of each breakpoint to the current clinically standardized transcript;

6. the approximate percentage of coding sequence removed from each breakpoint gene;

7. which noncoding RNAs and pseudogenes are contained within the interval;

8. which nearby loci should explicitly be excluded;

9. and which structural questions remain unresolved because the reported clinical

coordinates may

       not represent nucleotide-level chromosome junctions. This phase is deliberately restricted

to structural genomic characterization. No visual, neurological, developmental, or other
                                                                                                  5

phenotype is inferred from the presence of a gene inside the deletion. The final product of the

phase is designated:

17p13.3 Deletion Map v1.0

3. Coordinate System and Genome Assembly

3.1 Freeze the genome assembly

       The first requirement is to preserve the genome assembly exactly as reported. The index

finding uses:

GRCh37 / hg19

       For NCBI, the corresponding reference assembly is:

GRCh37.p13 — GCF_000001405.25

       NCBI maintains GRCh37.p13 annotation resources, while Ensembl maintains a dedicated

GRCh37 archive. The Ensembl GRCh37 gene annotation is based on an older frozen gene build,

whereas some variation and phenotype information continues to receive updates. Consequently,

Ensembl GRCh37 and current RefSeq/MANE annotations should not be expected to contain

identical transcript versions. (ncbi.nlm.nih.gov) The working rule for this study is therefore: Do

not mix GRCh37 and GRCh38 coordinates within a single structural calculation. If a source
                                                                                                   8

provides only GRCh38 coordinates, those coordinates must either be excluded from the GRCh37

calculation or deliberately converted using a documented assembly-conversion method.

3.2 Preserve the original clinical coordinates

        Create a source record containing: Assembly: GRCh37/hg19 Chromosome: 17 Start:

1,411,408 End: 1,841,103 Variant: deletion If the coordinates are treated as 1-based inclusive,

the nominal interval length is:

                            1, 841, 103 −1, 411, 408 + 1 = 429, 696 bp

        or approximately:

429.7 kb

        The clinical description of >430 kb should be preserved as reported rather than silently

changed, because clinical microarray boundaries may reflect probe resolution, reporting

conventions, or approximate interval designation. For calculations in this project, the explicit

genomic coordinates take precedence over the rounded verbal size.

4. Coordinate-Convention Control

        Coordinate conventions are a potential source of one-base replication errors. NCBI GFF3

annotation uses 1-based coordinates, with the start and end positions representing genomic

feature boundaries. (ncbi.nlm.nih.gov) BED-based formats used by UCSC and many

bioinformatics programs use a 0-based, half-open convention. (genome.ucsc.edu) Therefore the

1-based inclusive interval: chr17:1,411,408-1,841,103 would be represented in BED format as:

chr17

        17p13.3_index_deletion The original clinical coordinates should never be overwritten by

this conversion. Instead preserve both: Clinical/1-based: chr17:1411408-1841103 BED/0-based
                                                                                                   9

half-open: chr17 1411407 1841103 A peer attempting to reproduce the analysis should verify

which convention is expected by every software package before entering coordinates.

7. Identification of the Two Boundary Genes

        Using this interval method, two protein-coding genes intersect the deletion boundaries:

   INPP5K at the lower-coordinate boundary;

   RTN4RL1 at the upper-coordinate boundary.

        The remaining confirmed internal protein-coding genes fall completely inside the

overlap the deletion, but whether the overlap includes coding sequence.

8. Selecting the Reference Transcript

8.1 Why MANE Select is used

        Transcript selection is necessary because one gene may have multiple isoforms with

different exons and coding boundaries. The Matched Annotation from NCBI and EMBL-EBI

project (MANE) provides a standardized transcript pair in which the RefSeq and

Ensembl/GENCODE representatives are matched in exon structure and coding sequence. MANE

Select is intended to provide one representative transcript per locus and is widely useful for

clinical reporting and genomic research. (ncbi.nlm.nih.gov) For Phase 1, the rule is:

Use the current MANE Select transcript as the primary transcript-level reference

        whenever one is available. Alternative transcripts should be preserved separately rather

than substituted silently.

9. INPP5K Breakpoint Mapping

9.1 Establish genomic orientation

       NCBI places INPP5K on the minus strand. This is critical because transcript direction is

opposite increasing chromosome coordinates. For a minus-strand gene: higher genomic

coordinates = transcript 5′ side lower genomic coordinates

       = transcript 3′ side The deletion begins at: 1,411,408 inside INPP5K. The deleted portion

runs from that point toward the higher-coordinate end of the gene. Therefore: The index deletion

removes the 5′ portion of INPP5K.

26. Breakpoint-Resolution Limitation

       The strongest limitation in Phase 1 concerns the meaning of the clinical CNV endpoints.

The calculations treat: chr17:1,411,408-1,841,103 as the operational deleted interval. If these

values originated from chromosomal microarray analysis, they may represent the positions of the

last informative probes or another assay-defined boundary rather than the exact DNA junction.

Therefore the analysis establishes:
                                                                                               26

feature overlap at the resolution of the reported CNV

         but not necessarily: the exact nucleotide at which the chromosome physically broke and

rejoined. Consequently, statements such as: ~49.6% INPP5K coding loss ~99.0% RTN4RL1

CCDS loss must be qualified: under the reported-coordinate model. Nucleotide-resolution

confirmation would require an assay capable of identifying the physical breakpoint, such as

appropriate whole-genome sequencing, targeted breakpoint sequencing, long-read sequencing, or

another validated breakpoint-resolution method.

31. Phase 1 Conclusion

Phase 1 establishes the index CNV as a structurally defined interstitial 17p13.3 deletion

rather

         than a generic 17p13.3 syndrome interval. The most important structural findings are that

both breakpoint genes lose coding sequence and that their functional coding disruption is

substantially different from what genomic-span overlap alone would imply. INPP5K loses

approximately half of its MANE protein-coding sequence from the affected chromosome under

the reported-coordinate model. RTN4RL1 presents an even more pronounced discrepancy

between genomic-span overlap and coding disruption: although the deletion intersects only a

small proportion of the total genomic locus, it encompasses approximately 99% of the relevant

CCDS nucleotide sequence. The internal interval completely encompasses at least eleven

additional protein-coding genes, three established noncoding RNA genes, and one RNA

pseudogene. The resulting minimum map contains:
