1. Purpose and Phase 6 Status

         Phase 6 concludes the first-pass in silico research program. It does not reopen or silently

revise the frozen findings of Phases 1-5. Its role is to consolidate what the project established,

identify what remains unsupported or unresolved, critically assess the methodology and

inferential limits, define two legitimate continuation paths, and establish a version-controlled

stopping point from which future work can proceed.

         The project began with a broad visual-neurological question, including possible

photosensitivity and visually triggered seizure mechanisms. The accumulated evidence narrowed

that question. The strongest defensible interpretation is now a distributed visual-neural

functional-reserve model involving retinal/RPE support, membrane and phosphoinositide

biology, endosomal/lysosomal trafficking, genome-stress response, neural development, and

network organization. The exact heterozygous CNV has not been shown to cause abnormal

visual-cortical flicker processing, a photoparoxysmal response, or visually triggered seizures.

3. Integrated Findings of the First-Pass Research

3.1 Structural genomics

       Phase 1 converted a generic 17p13.3 label into a reproducible locus-specific substrate.

The main structural result was that apparent genomic-span overlap can substantially understate

coding disruption at the two boundary genes. The project therefore correctly anchored all later

interpretation to the exact reported interval rather than importing phenotypes from broader

17p13.3 deletion syndromes.

3.2 Molecular and biochemical architecture

       Phase 2 showed that the interval is not biologically featureless. Multiple affected genes

occupy mechanistically relevant systems: phosphoinositide metabolism, phospholipid transfer

and membrane composition, late-endosome and lysosome biology, intracellular transport, retinal

trophic support, RNA splicing, DNA replication and repair, neuronal structural signaling, amino-

acid transport, immune clearance, and fibrinolysis. The strongest mechanistic convergence

involved INPP5K, PITPNA, TLCD2, WDR81, RILP, and SERPINF1, with RTN4RL1 adding
                                                                                                 252

neuronal surface and structural signaling. This was evidence of functional adjacency and

convergence, not proof of multigene synergy or epistasis.

3.3 Spatial and developmental opportunity

       Phase 3 established where affected mechanisms could plausibly operate. The clearest

direct cell-localized convergence was RPE-centered, particularly INPP5K, PITPNA, and

SERPINF1. PRPF8 showed broad retinal relevance, and other loci provided distributed retinal,

neuronal, cortical, and developmental opportunity. The negative results were equally

informative: the affected interval did not emerge as a preferential magnocellular program in

human dLGN data, and adult/developmental cortical analyses did not support a simple V1-

specific expression model. Expression was explicitly treated as opportunity rather than

dysfunction.

3.4 Dosage, perturbation, and phenotype-level evidence

       Phase 4 moved beyond expression to perturbational evidence and genotype-state

discipline. RPA1 provided the clearest direct human haploinsufficiency precedent; SERPINF1

showed direct heterozygous molecular dosage reduction of PEDF; INPP5K provided one-copy

physiological evidence outside the retina together with strong phosphoinositide/lysosomal

mechanism. Other loci supplied strong but less genotype-equivalent evidence from biallelic loss,

knockout, hypomorphs, or variant-specific disease mechanisms. The project repeatedly preserved

the distinction between severe loss and heterozygous deletion.

       At the systems level, the retinal evidence favored impaired maintenance, reduced

resilience, delayed recovery, or stress vulnerability more strongly than increased sensory gain.

The best partial-dose retinal physiology identified in the project was reduced, not amplified,

electrophysiological response. Likewise, RTN4RL1 complete-loss neural models altered network
                                                                                                   253

construction but did not support a generic hyperexcitability narrative. MIR22 remained relevant

to epileptogenesis, but its strongest evidence came from complete loss after an epileptogenic

insult, not from heterozygous loss under photic stimulation.

3.5 Phase 5 deterministic integration and simulation

        Phase 5 formalized the earlier record into a layered evidence model and treated missing

downstream physiology as missing rather than as zero or as implied support. Its modeling and

simulation work did not create biological evidence; it examined methodological behavior. The

standardized simulation suite used 20,000 replicates per scenario/cell where applicable,

approximately 1.88 million primary stochastic draws/resamples. It showed that modest

heterozygous effects can be difficult to detect with too few independent biological replicates, that

pseudoreplication cannot substitute for independent clones or donors, that broad frequency scans

can generate false-positive signals without correction, and that limited data can make a

distributed mechanism appear spuriously single-gene.

        The integrated Phase 5 conclusion remained conservative: the deletion is best treated as a

candidate distributed visual-neural reserve lesion, not a proven single-gene photosensitivity

mutation. Broader retinal/RPE dysfunction, developmental/network modification, and stress-

dependent vulnerability are better supported than photic seizure causation.

5. Critical Assessment of the Work

5.1 What the project did well

           It froze a precise genomic reference and resisted syndrome-level overgeneralization from

larger 17p13.3 deletions containing different genes.
                                                                                                  255

       It enforced an evidence hierarchy: deletion -> dosage -> molecular perturbation -> spatial

opportunity -> cellular/developmental effect -> visual physiology -> phenotype -> causality.

       It distinguished genotype states rather than treating knockout, biallelic loss, pathogenic

variants, and heterozygous deletion as interchangeable.

       It preserved counterevidence and null findings, including compensation, normal

heterozygous states, reduced rather than increased response, and failure to identify magnocellular

or V1-specific signatures.

       It progressively narrowed the working hypothesis instead of escalating claim strength as

more literature accumulated.

       Phase 5 explicitly addressed reproducibility hazards such as low independent replicate

count, pseudoreplication, multiple-frequency testing, and model simplification.

5.2 Where the methodology remains vulnerable

       Literature dependence. Much of the mechanistic evidence is assembled across different

species, tissues, ages, perturbations, and assay types. Biological coherence can be real while still

failing to transfer quantitatively to this exact CNV.

       Sparse exact-genotype evidence. Most loci lack direct measurements in cells carrying the

complete heterozygous deletion. This is the largest limitation in converting mechanism into

CNV-specific biology.

       Potential ascertainment and confirmation bias. The project began from a visual-

neurological question, which creates a risk that visual or seizure-relevant literature was more

deeply sampled than unrelated phenotypic domains. The counterevidence framework reduces but

cannot fully remove that risk.
                                                                                                256

       Pathway and network inflation. Gene-dense CNVs can appear convergent because

broadly expressed genes participate in common cellular systems. Computational convergence

cannot substitute for direct combinatorial perturbation.

       Transcript and breakpoint uncertainty. The array-defined boundaries do not determine the

exact chromosome junction, transcript fate, nonsense-mediated decay, promoter/enhancer

effects, or allele-specific compensation for INPP5K and RTN4RL1.

       Expression-resource limitations. Single-cell and single-nucleus atlases have dropout,

donor, sampling, batch, regional-dissection, developmental-stage, and annotation constraints;

expression cannot establish dysfunction.

       Model dependence. Even disciplined ordinal or deterministic evidence models depend on

chosen weights, equivalence penalties, and missingness rules. Robustness analyses help identify

sensitivity but cannot convert sparse biology into calibrated causal probability.

5.3 Adversarial interpretation

       A skeptical reading can explain much of the current record without invoking

photosensitivity: the CNV may be biologically consequential because it affects several broadly

important cellular systems, while the apparent visual-neural convergence may reflect a mixture

of genuine retinal relevance, broad neuronal expression, and literature availability. That

null/weak-specificity alternative has not been eliminated. Conversely, the persistence of

retinal/RPE, trafficking, stress-response, and network-development signals across independent

evidence types argues against treating the interval as biologically irrelevant. The balanced

conclusion is therefore neither “causal” nor “nothing”: the deletion remains a credible candidate

reserve lesion whose disease-specific physiological consequences are unmeasured.

6. Evidence Boundary and Falsifiability

         The highest supported causal level is: structurally defined heterozygous CNV with

selected demonstrated dosage consequences, mechanistically relevant retinal/neural pathways,

spatiotemporal opportunity, constituent-gene molecular/cellular phenotypes, and distributed

functional convergence. The project does not cross the required bridge into exact-CNV retinal

physiology, thalamic transfer, V1 flicker physiology, cortical inhibitory containment,

pathological frequency entrainment, long-range propagation, PPR, or visually triggered seizure

causation.

         Evidence that would materially weaken the functional-reserve model includes: normal

molecular and physiological behavior of an isogenic full-CNV heterozygous system across the

relevant retinal/neural assays under adequate power; complete compensation of predicted

dosage-sensitive nodes; absence of stress-dependent differences after calibrated challenge; or

demonstration that matched genomic control intervals show equal or stronger domain-specific

convergence under the same analytical pipeline. Evidence that would strengthen the model

requires exact-genotype perturbation and, ideally, rescue.

7. Path A - Continue In Silico Research with Mentorship and Peer Exposure

         This path treats the first-pass record as a reproducible research portfolio to be stress-

tested by domain experts rather than as a finished causal claim. The near-term objective is

methodological exposure: place the frozen pipeline, evidence tables, assumptions, and negative

findings in front of mentors or peers in human genetics, retinal biology, neurophysiology,

bioinformatics, or epilepsy research and invite attempts to reproduce, criticize, or break the

model.
                                                                                                 258

       Convert the Phase 1-5 evidence base into a clean public or mentor-reviewable repository

with frozen inputs, source manifest, scripts, environment specification, and explicit expected

outputs.

       Ask a mentor or peer to independently audit a bounded component first - for example the

CNV structural map, RPE convergence, genotype-equivalence coding, dLGN/V1 interpretation,

or Phase 5 statistical assumptions.

       Reanalyze one or two public datasets from raw or minimally processed data under

preregistered questions, rather than adding more narrative literature synthesis.

       Seek external review of the null model and the phenotype-ascertainment problem:

whether an unrelated gene-dense CNV would appear similarly “convergent” under the same

workflow.

       Present negative and contradictory findings alongside positive results. The project should

gain credibility by surviving criticism, not by maximizing the number of supportive mechanisms.

       Use mentor feedback to define a Version 2 research protocol. Do not alter this frozen

Version 1 record; any changes should be explicit amendments or a new project pass.

       A useful endpoint for Path A would be a mentor-reviewed methods note, reproducible

notebook/repository, poster, undergraduate research presentation, or small methods manuscript

that focuses on evidence integration and rare-CNV hypothesis discipline rather than claiming a

new clinical syndrome.

8. Path B - Transition Toward Wet-Lab Research

8.1 Brief research proposal

       Provisional title: Functional Validation of a Heterozygous 17p13.3 Microdeletion as a

Distributed Retinal-Neural Reserve Lesion.
                                                                                              259

       Central question: Does the exact heterozygous chr17:1,411,408-1,841,103 deletion

produce measurable, rescue-sensitive deficits in retinal/RPE maintenance, membrane/endosomal

biology, stress response, neural development, or visually driven network dynamics?

       Primary hypothesis: The CNV produces modest, distributed deficits in retinal/RPE and

neural functional reserve that may be compensated at baseline but become measurable under

controlled physiological or stress challenge. Photosensitivity is a downstream exploratory

phenotype, not the primary assumed outcome.

       Alternative/null hypotheses: (1) the heterozygous CNV is substantially compensated and

produces no reproducible phenotype under the tested conditions; (2) one locus dominates the

phenotype; (3) phenotypes are developmental/network-specific without retinal involvement; or

(4) retinal abnormalities occur without abnormal cortical flicker propagation.

8.2 Specific aims

       Create or obtain isogenic wild-type, heterozygous full-CNV, corrected/rescued, and

selected single-gene heterozygous human cellular lines. Confirm CNV structure and

transcript/protein dosage before phenotyping.

       Test retinal/RPE reserve using retinal organoids and/or RPE models. Priority endpoints:

phosphoinositide pools, lipid composition, outer-segment handling, lysosomal/endosomal

function, oxidative/DNA-damage response, dark-adaptation or electrophysiologic analogues, and

recovery after calibrated challenge.

       Test neural/network reserve in relevant human neuronal systems. Priority endpoints:

progenitor behavior, neurite morphology, synapse density, E/I-associated measures, calcium

dynamics, multielectrode-array firing/bursting, frequency entrainment, phase locking, and long-

range propagation.
                                                                                                  260

       Use genotype correction or targeted rescue to distinguish CNV-specific effects from

background variation and to identify whether observed phenotypes are distributed or locus-

dominant.

8.3 Minimal experimental design principles

       Use independent biological clones/donors; do not treat many cells from one clone as

independent replication.

       Predefine primary endpoints and flicker-frequency analyses; control family-wise or false-

discovery error across frequency scans and multiple phenotypes.

       Blind image/trace quantification where practicable and preserve raw data.

       Report null results and directionally contradictory effects.

       Power studies for modest heterozygous effects rather than knockout-scale effects.

       Treat rescue as a major causal discriminator.

8.4 Funding and fellowship strategy

       The most realistic transition is mentor- and institution-based. A trainee should first join or

collaborate with a laboratory that already has the required cell culture, retinal organoid/RPE,

electrophysiology, imaging, or network-analysis infrastructure. The project can then be adapted

to the host laboratory rather than attempting to acquire an entire platform de novo.

       NIH/NEI training mechanisms such as F31 or F32 are appropriate once the investigator is

in the corresponding predoctoral or postdoctoral training stage and working with an eligible

mentor/institution.

       NIH R15/AREA mechanisms can support small-scale biomedical projects at eligible

undergraduate-focused institutions, but the principal investigator must satisfy institutional and
                                                                                                261

appointment requirements; this is better viewed as a mentor/institutional route than an immediate

independent application.

       Vision-focused foundations can be relevant when the proposal is framed around retinal

cell biology or inherited retinal mechanisms; epilepsy foundations become more appropriate only

if the project directly tests seizure/network physiology rather than relying on indirect mechanistic

relevance.

       The grant pitch should emphasize the unusual strength of the preliminary in silico work

as a hypothesis-narrowing and assay-prioritization package, while explicitly stating that the

central physiological claims remain unvalidated. The strongest proposal is one that funds the

missing measurements, not one that presents the missing measurements as already solved.

9. Recommended Decision Point

       The two paths are complementary rather than mutually exclusive. The immediate low-

cost continuation is Path A: external methodological scrutiny, replication, and mentorship. Path

B becomes appropriate when a host laboratory, mentor, and assay platform can support exact-

genotype experiments. The first wet-lab study should be deliberately narrower than the full

project and should target one or two mechanistic bottlenecks with high discriminating value.

       A particularly efficient first experiment would compare isogenic wild-type, full-CNV

heterozygous, and corrected cells in a retinal/RPE model under baseline and calibrated stress. If

no reproducible molecular or physiological deficit is detected with adequate power, the

distributed reserve hypothesis should be weakened. If a deficit is detected and rescued, the

project gains a direct CNV-specific causal foothold before moving toward more complex

thalamic/cortical photic models.

10. Final Project Conclusion

       The first-pass in silico program establishes a structurally precise, biologically meaningful

17p13.3 heterozygous deletion containing multiple loci with credible dosage, retinal, membrane,

trafficking, stress-response, and neurodevelopmental relevance. Across the project, the evidence

repeatedly converged on reduced or altered functional reserve rather than on a single-gene or

simple hyperexcitable-retina mechanism. The strongest phenotype-level support concerns

retinal/RPE maintenance, photoreceptor integrity, intracellular material handling, stress

resilience, and neural-development/network organization. General epileptogenic modification

remains plausible but incompletely genotype-matched. Epileptic photosensitivity remains

downstream of an unmeasured physiological gap.

       Accordingly, the project does not conclude that the CNV causes photosensitivity, a

photoparoxysmal response, or visually triggered seizures. It concludes that the CNV is a credible

candidate distributed visual-neural reserve lesion whose most important unresolved questions

now require either independent methodological scrutiny or direct experimental perturbation. This

narrowing is the principal scientific accomplishment of the first pass.
